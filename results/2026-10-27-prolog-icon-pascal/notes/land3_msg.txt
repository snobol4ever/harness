THE COROUTINE'S TRAIL ENTRIES ARE ANOTHER OWNER'S WHILE IT IS PARKED. A reset/3 goal runs as a coroutine on the shared trail; when it parks (a shift, or its goal exits) its choices' sentinels and bindings stay on the trail above the caller's choice. The caller's own commits then tidied them away: a cut, an if-then-else commit or a last call compacts the trail from its choice's mark and drops every sentinel and every binding newer than that choice, so `r(G) :- reset(G, _, _), ( true ; true )`-shaped callers lost the coroutine's choice points and a redo into reset/3 found nothing (findall(X, reset(member(X,[1,2,3]),_,_), L) gave [1]; the bisect reaches back before the LIFO work, where the same program crashed). The tidy already starts at max(the choice's mark, the activation's trail floor, PL_TR_FLOOR_OFF), the floor rt_plco_resume writes for a resumed continuation; a context now also keeps pl_keep, the trail top of the newest child it saw park alive, and every floor write is max(pl_floor, pl_keep). pl_keep is clamped to the caller's trail top when the caller has backtracked below it (erring high only costs tidying). The park fence reads the context's own pl_floor, which the slot carried before, so the continuation fence is unchanged.
